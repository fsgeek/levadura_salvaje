# What the ledger says so far (2026-09-24)

A synthesis of obs-0001..0145. Every number below comes from a ledger entry
and a scorecard, and each measurement was scored against predictions stamped
before it ran. The scorecards have the detail and the failures:
[AMT lens](amt-lens-scorecard.md), [fossils](fossil-scorecard.md),
[reuse and moves](reuse-scorecard.md), [currency](currency-scorecard.md),
[dated periods](dated-scorecard.md).

## The corpus

- **26 CFR**, the 1997 and 2025 editions: 4,983 and 6,158 sections. An
  edition is not a single moment: 2025 holds volumes dated 2020, 2024 and
  2025.
- **26 USC**, at release points 119-4 (2025-03-15, in force on the 2025
  CFR's date) and 119-110 (2026-09-16): 57,176 and 58,388 identified
  provisions. OBBBA falls between the two.

## Findings, in the order they build

1. **Regulations outlive their statute, a lot.** 125k Code citations in the
   2025 CFR. **One in five distinct cited Code sections is repealed or
   missing** at 119-4, and **27% of sections** (1,675) cite at least one
   dead provision. A blind audit confirms 94% of flagged citations
   (extractor v2). The dead span seventy years: the median repeal year of a
   cited dead section is 2000, 61% were repealed more than 20 years ago, and
   §§452/462, repealed in 1955, are still cited 126 times. Each major tax act
   adds a layer, and the layers stay.
   **And the rate is stationary.** Read against the statute of its own day
   (GPO's edition current through 1997-01-06), the 1997 CFR looks the same:
   19.0% of cited sections dead (2025: 20.1%), 28.1% of sections fossil
   candidates (2025: 27.2%). The corpus carries a steady load of dead
   citations, not a growing one. It stays steady through persistence, not
   renewal. 84% of 1997 fossils that survive to 2025 are still fossils, and
   615 of those were amended in between without losing their dead
   citations.

2. **The dead mostly don't say they're dead.** Judged from their own text,
   **about 91%** of those 1,675 read as current law, meaning they state at
   least one rule with no time limit. About half name *some* period that has
   ended, often in one clause among current-looking rules. **About two in
   five give no textual cue at all.** The AMT fossils that obs-0121 marked
   "governs no current year" read as current from their text (13 of 14).
   That verdict came from knowledge of the 1986 repeal, not from the words.

3. **Existence checks overstate health, because numbers get reused.** In 18
   months the statute changed 110 headings under unchanged identifiers. 37
   of them are re-letterings the ledger can follow, such as §951A(c) → (b)
   and §4968(b)-(d) → (c), (f), (g). **134 CFR citations now resolve to a
   different rule than the one they were written for.** Revived numbers do
   the same: §168(n) and §6659 came back as new law, and a 1954-Code citation
   of §39 (gasoline) "resolves" to today's §39 (credit carrybacks). An audit
   control caught that one by accident.
   Over 28 years (1997 to 2025), 42 section numbers changed meaning: 31 were
   reused for a different subject and 11 restructured. **293 regulations
   cite one of them, and 147 of those have no broken citation, so an
   existence check calls them healthy.**

4. **The instrument has a measurable noise floor.** Jev, pinned to one
   version and given byte-identical input, moved its probabilities on 7% of
   1,688 repeated questions and flipped 2 labels, both near a tie. The currency
   and dated lenses built on it held up under blind audit: 93% and 95% agreement.

5. **A small reader does better with a slice of the ledger, reliably.**
   v2 repeated the test on 120 fresh sections with a panel that never saw
   the packet: 82.5% text-only, 90.8% with evidence, 13 fixes against 3 new
   errors (p = 0.021). All the gain is on fossil candidates. v1 (below) had
   the circularity:
   Haiku 4.5, asked whether a section still operates in 2025: 82% from the
   text alone, 90% with a ~2,000-character evidence packet (6 fixes, 1 new
   error). But most fossils aren't dead: an Opus panel says 70% of unmarked
   fossils still state a live rule. A dead citation usually means a stale
   reference inside live law, not a dead rule. The dead sections both
   readers missed were ones the ledger can't see: reused numbers (old §683)
   and programs that ended while their citations stayed valid.

## What the predictions taught me about my own priors

Across the 71 scored predictions in these scorecards (46 pass: bounded reader 1/5, bounded reader v2 3/5, 28-year reuse 3/3, AMT 12/15, fossils 6/9, fossil age 3/4, 1997 fossils 2/5, turnover 4/5, reuse 4/5, moves 2/3, currency 2/7, dated 4/5), the misses
cluster:

- **I underestimated persistence, mistook it for accumulation, then
  mistook it for death** (B5: 70% of unmarked fossils still operate). I predicted 6% dead cited sections
  (actual 20%) and 55% current-reading fossils (actual 90%). My prior was
  drawn from the AMT area and assumed the regulations are maintained. They
  are only amended.
- **I made the same per-section mistake twice** (P5, D4). Sections
  unchanged since 1997 are short, so per-section rates reverse the
  per-citation direction. When the unit is a section, control for length.
- **I wrote one instrument definition that a sentence I didn't mean could
  satisfy** (R3: "absent at 119-4" included every new provision).
- **I stated a premise from memory that was wrong** (C5: "the AMT sections
  said so plainly in their text"). The audit caught it. Premises in
  pre-registrations need checking too, not just predictions.

## What this is for

The project's claim is that a small mind doesn't need to hold the evidence,
only ways to observe it and return. This corpus is a clean case of why that
matters. The regulations can't be read at face value. Whether a rule is alive
lives *outside* its text: in the statute, in the dates of both, and in the
history of every number. A reader holding only the regulation, human or
model, gets it wrong about two times in five, and never knows.
